# Closing Time: bounded FE09 story review

13 October 2026 · One Astra Ultra judgement pass · Source baseline: PR34 merge `261fec1eec36bb2300719e3dcc698c9c640feb37`; checked runtime/export remains beta.16 at `a7e5ed4b653823ee2a84cb6df84d425206c46072`.

**Finish the promise to save the bakery before expanding the mystery.** FE08 now establishes a place, a delivery and people whose rescue changes the next encounter. The later campaign still diverts into an examination and ends when a breakable frees an unnamed soldier. There is no player action that stops the siege bowl and no final return home. This is a bounded implementation recommendation, not human story acceptance or a claim that the game is finished.

## Five highest-impact findings

Priorities below concern the FE09 commitment. A P1 missing chapter requirement is not a claim of a newly reproduced crash or progression bug. This pass inspected source and retained captures/reports; it ran no game, tests, soaks or generation.

### 1. The chapter awards its ending before the bakery is saved

**P1 — missing authored scope; design judgement about closure.** In [campaign.js](../game/campaign.js), `rescue()` at line 71 starts the last objective; the Rind crate event at line 200 immediately calls `finish(true)`. Lines 154–166 then save spoons and publish the completed result. There is no sluice phase or diversion state. Rind's node outro (line 15) and theatre outro ([story-scenes.js](../game/story-scenes.js), line 21) end with the missing Spoon and “This chapter ends here.” The completed map's message ([campaign-ui.js](../game/campaign-ui.js), line 41) advertises an open rice road rather than showing home safe.

**Player impact:** the threatened bakery is the reason to act, but the player's last action resolves another problem elsewhere. The larger mystery receives emphasis before the local promise is paid off.

**Smallest useful change:** extend the existing `rind` node with a short sluice objective after the rescue. The recruit clears the jam and operates the winch; the channel opens and the bowl visibly diverts into a catch basin, clear of the bakery. Award Rind's result only then. Follow with a distinct, non-awarding playable homecoming before the normal result panel. Do not add a ninth node or another boss/wave ladder.

**Verification:** before the final winch action, Rind's first spoon and chapter milestone remain unearned on a fresh isolated save. One completed action visibly changes channel/bowl state and awards once. Death/quit/reload from the finale resumes there, not at the boss. Homecoming, skip, scene replay and repeated result activation cannot award again. An uncoached player should be able to describe what they did to save home; that observation is pending.

### 2. The mandatory middle interrupts the threat and makes Stirling its obstacle

**P1 — source-confirmed structure; design judgement.** The dependency chain in [campaign.js](../game/campaign.js), lines 10–15, forces guard → bridge → Stirling → Steve. `testPhase()` (65–69), Stirling's tick (178) and objective (208) require three blocks against Stirling, defeating Stirling, then holding lunch. Bridge's theatre outro explicitly announces an examination ([story-scenes.js](../game/story-scenes.js), line 18). `grid()` supplies a blank shared arena; bridge adds gap columns/ice and gates, while guard/Stirling/Steve lack authored service-route scenery (campaign lines 61, 146–150, 213).

**Player impact:** the recruit has just saved cooks from a siege, yet is required to demonstrate qualifications and protect a pie. The next place follows the node list rather than the visible problem. Renaming “tests” to “support” while retaining a compulsory block count would leave that problem intact.

**Smallest useful change:** make the defended bakery reveal a membrane-choked cooling channel. The previously met Rice guard controls the service crossing; beating their shield opens that route. Bridge becomes the cooling channel with readable dry landings and the existing safe outer path. Reframe `stirling` as moving the cooks' tools through the far approach: Stirling holds pursuers at one entrance, the player advances the tools, clears a shield raider at the access obstruction, then holds the tool position while service access opens. Keep three resumable stages, but remove the mandatory block quota and the duel against Stirling. Blocking/parrying remains useful and an optional spoon, not a permission exam. Steve's food trail then supplies the reason to take the newly opened service route to the winch.

**Verification:** a normal run can finish Stirling without recording three blocks; Stirling is visibly helping and is never the required damage target. Earlier work makes the next gate accessible. Bridge remains finishable without the optional dash spoon or Biscuit lesson. Copied Stirling phase-1/phase-2 saves reconstruct tools and support already in place without replaying earlier stages. Observe whether a fresh player can name the destination and why they need it before leaving the bakery.

### 3. Rind cannot visibly witness a recognisable person's rescue in the current fight

**P1 — source-confirmed staging gap; design judgement about alliance.** `guard` spawns “Rice guard” and finishes when no enemies remain (campaign lines 146, 175). Rind's threshold starts the shield-wall phase, and only `b.dead` starts `rescue()` (70–71, 180). That routine marks all nonhuman combat entities dead and creates a crate labelled “Trapped rice soldier.” Phase 2 continues to attack with warned skin lashes (181); the civilian renderer at 225–234 applies to banquet cooks, not this soldier. Rind and a freed guard reappear in the *theatre* outro (story-scenes line 72), not as live witnesses during the rescue. Engine `dead` here is a combat KO flag, not evidence of a narrative fatality.

**Player impact:** the moral turning point is a labelled breakable and later explanation. The player cannot connect a recognisable previous opponent to the person they save or see Rind change behaviour because of it.

**Smallest useful change:** give the existing crossing guard one stable identity, name cue and a modest recognisable shield detail; use it again at the winch. Preserve Rind's two readable combat phases. At his nonfatal defeat, lower his weapon and keep a visibly breathing/staggered Rind present as scenery or a neutral actor. Clear active attacks/projectiles/warnings and stop the countdown during the safe rescue interval. Show the guard trapped behind the actual membrane breakable. The recruit cuts it; the guard moves clear; Rind sees them and takes a support position before control proceeds to the sluice. The rescue must remain playable and cannot be skipped by a story button.

**Deliberate bounded variation from the earlier treatment:** place the safe rescue between the two-phase boss fight and the cooperative finale, rather than between Rind's two attack phases. This preserves existing checkpoint meanings and avoids making Rind attack the recruit again immediately after recognising the rescue. No second speech or compulsory morality choice is needed.

**Verification:** the guard's earlier identity and rescue identity match; Rind remains visible and does not attack during rescue; waiting through several former lash intervals causes no damage or time-out. Leaving/retrying the legacy soldier-rescue checkpoint still performs that rescue and does not repeat the boss. Human recognition of the guard and Rind's changed allegiance remains an observation to obtain.

### 4. Story routes use older scenes that contradict the revised play, and terminal wins lack a default consequence

**P2 — reproducible source inconsistency and routing gap, not an observed soft lock.** The exact normal FE08 return route is `onAftermath` → `ui.brief('puddings',false)` ([campaign-integration.js](../game/campaign-integration.js), line 8). `brief()` passes the new node dialogue to `storyCard()` (UI line 71), but when `CKStoryScenes.duration` exists, lines 54–65 render the theatre and read its captions instead of those passed lines. Consequently the revised Mabel/Alf/Pip supply explanation is followed by the old generic “Save the puddings”/chairs scene and three anonymous outdoor tables (story-scenes lines 15, 67). Puddings' default victory continuation likewise shows the old banquet-table investigation before the next briefing (UI line 73). Changing `campaign.js` dialogue alone cannot repair this.

There are two further edges to address in that same presentation contract: natural bakery delivery launches banquet combat directly (integration line 8), so the first-time player does not see its existing pie/theft theatre introduction; and a win with `r.next === null` has no Continue action (UI line 73). The latter includes the current final Rind result and replays after all main nodes are complete. Its aftermath is available only through “Replay aftermath scene.”

**Player impact:** named home/supplies turn back into generic props; an inciting event is omitted on one entry route; and the final consequence is treated as optional archive content. Showing both old outro and next intro also inserts repeated pauses between short actions.

**Smallest useful change:** align both node text and actual theatre cues/rendering in the PR that changes each beat. Give first entry from delivery one short skippable banquet disruption transition; retain direct combat retry. Make normal continuation carry one concise consequence and the next actionable destination, without requiring two full scene cards where they repeat the same information. Keep all retained theatre material available from replay. Add an explicit final homecoming route; do not reuse banquet's `aftermath`, whose completion/skip is hardwired to Puddings.

**Verification:** follow natural delivery, explicit opening skip, direct retry, normal victory Continue, final win, map return and theatre replay separately. Record the actual caption/scene ID and destination for each. Back/skip/completion must stop the old scene and invoke exactly one destination. No map re-entry or replay starts a stale pending encounter. Pacing and whether the short disruption communicates “why the rescue” still need people.
